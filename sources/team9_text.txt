Benchmarking QAOA across Diverse Graph Structures

SLIDE 2
Problem Definition
Benchmarking QAOA to solve MaxCut Problem across eight graph structures: Path, Star, Cycle, Tree, Complete, Bipartite, Community, and Random.
Examine how graph structure and QAOA depth (P = 1, 2, 3) affect solution quality and optimization stability.
Evaluate trade-offs among circuit depth, solution quality, noise sensitivity, and gate cost.
Our Approach
Established exact MaxCut references using brute force for 10-node graphs and SCIP for larger instances.
Ran complete 10-node experiments using Ideal Aer, IBM-calibrated Noisy Aer, Heron, and Nighthawk.
Measured approximation ratio, optimal-solution probability, convergence stability, noise loss, circuit depth, and two qubit gate count.
Compared Heron, Nighthawk, and QOPT-optimized Heron to evaluate hardware topology and routing efficiency.
Research Goal
Identify which graph structures and QAOA depths provide a balance between solution quality, robustness, and hardware execution cost.
Determine when classical simulation becomes impractical and whether hardware topology and QAOA-specific routing can reduce circuit depth and noise-induced performance loss.

Key Finding
Nighthawk reduced depth and 2-qubit gates, but improved quality in only 4 of 6 cases.
QOPT reduced depth more substantially and improved quality in all 6 cases.
A shallower circuit does not guarantee better results because performance also depends on gate fidelity, readout error, coherence, and calibration.
